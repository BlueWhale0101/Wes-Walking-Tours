Stop Diagnostic

Visible Anchor

• Railway station or station frontage.
• Rail corridor, tracks, platform edge, fences, road crossing, and Railway Terrace alignment.
• Nearby railway cottages or industrial/service edges if field-visible.
• Freight/tourism traces, signage, station names, or Ghan branding if present.
• Sightlines toward the town grid, Todd River crossing direction, and ranges/gaps.

Core System

Inland logistics: camel transport, goldfield supply, railway extension, wartime movement, road competition, and tourism rail. The stop should explain how Alice Springs changed when a railhead arrived, but also show that rail did not begin the supply story.

Central Pressure

Before rail, Afghan cameleers and camel trains carried provisions from Oodnadatta and beyond into a hot, sparsely supplied interior. Rail mechanised part of that supply chain in 1929, but the line remained exposed to heat, flood, and distance, and the full Adelaide-Darwin dream was not completed until 2004.

Human Pressure

• Afghan cameleers are central to the transport story and should be named as skilled logistics workers, not used only as an origin story for a train nickname.
• Alice Springs Town Council connects Arltunga gold, Oodnadatta railhead, camel supply, and the 1929 railway extension.
• Journey Beyond Rail dates the first Ghan departure from Adelaide to 4 August 1929, carrying more than 100 passengers and supplies to Stuart.
• The line was used heavily in WWII; Journey Beyond says 1944 was its busiest year with up to 247 trains per week.

Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. Arltunga gold was discovered in 1887 about 110 km east of Alice Springs.
2. Alice Springs Town Council says camel trains carried essential provisions 600 km from the railhead at Oodnadatta.
3. The camel trains carried water and supplies onward from Alice Springs toward Arltunga.
4. The Arltunga journey followed the East MacDonnell Ranges for about 120 km.
5. Council says that journey took at least a week in temperatures frequently exceeding 40 C.
6. Afghan cameleers drove camel trains through the desert climate and contributed to inland transport, mining, and pastoral logistics.
7. By 1929, the railway line from Oodnadatta was extended to Stuart.
8. Council describes the 1929 extension as a mechanised steam train replacing Afghan camel trains.
9. The first “Afghan Express” left Adelaide Railway Station on Sunday 4 August 1929.
10. It arrived in informally named “Alice Springs” on Tuesday 6 August 1929.
11. Journey Beyond says the first train carried more than 100 passengers and supplies bound for Stuart.
12. The train was steam hauled and contended with flash flooding and intense heat.
13. Journey Beyond says the Ghan line transported servicemen for training and deployment during WWII.
14. Journey Beyond says 1944 was the busiest year in the railway’s history, with up to 247 trains per week.
15. The Adelaide-Darwin transcontinental railway was completed in February 2004.
16. Council says the current full journey covers 2,979 km in 54 hours.
17. Journey Beyond says the inaugural transcontinental journey began on 1 February 2004.
18. The current track replaced the Old Ghan route through flood-prone regions.
19. The station/corridor should be used to read rail as an alignment through a dry, flood-prone, hot landscape rather than just transport nostalgia.

Script Spine

Open by having the listener look along the rails, fence, station frontage, and road/track alignment. Begin before the train: Arltunga gold, Oodnadatta, 600 km of camel supply, 120 km east into extreme heat. Then let the 1929 Afghan Express arrive as a change in logistics rather than a cute train story. Use WWII and the 2004 transcontinental completion to show how long the “north-south rail” ambition took. End by pointing toward Anzac Hill, where the listener can see the town, rail, road, river, and ranges in one field of view.

What The Script Must Avoid

• Do not reduce Afghan cameleers to the train nickname.
• Do not become a rail enthusiast timeline detached from visible infrastructure.
• Do not ignore flood and heat just because this is a transport stop.
• Do not overclaim current freight/tourism operations without checking station use.
• Do not use inaccessible station interiors as required evidence.
