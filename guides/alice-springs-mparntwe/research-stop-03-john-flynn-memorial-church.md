# Research Pack 03: John Flynn Memorial Church

Working research packet for Stop 3 of the Alice Springs / Mparntwe walking guide. This is not a script. It is the evidence base and field diagnostic to use before drafting.

## Stop Working Title

John Flynn Memorial Church: A Memorial Built for the Inland

## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- Church exterior beside Adelaide House.
- Todd Mall frontage and pedestrian setting.
- Plaques, foundation stone, or dedication text if visible.
- Masonry, roofline, shade, entry threshold, and church scale.
- Direct spatial relationship to Adelaide House and nearby civic heritage buildings.

### Core System

Civic memory, church networks, memorial architecture, remote medicine, and institutional authority. This stop should show how a practical inland service became a town-centre memorial building, not retell all of Adelaide House.

### Central Pressure

The same Australian Inland Mission and Presbyterian network associated with hospital, radio, and flying-doctor work built a permanent memorial church in the 1950s. The visible question is how a remote-service project becomes architecture, ritual, public memory, and institutional presence in the middle of town.

### Human Pressure

- Arthur Philpott designed the church.
- Prime Minister R. G. Menzies laid the foundation stone in June 1954.
- The church officially opened on 5 May 1956.
- The building was erected by the Presbyterian Church as a memorial to Rev John Flynn.
- Sir William Slim, Governor-General of Australia, gave an address at the opening dedication according to church-history sources.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. The NT Heritage Register lists the John Flynn Memorial Church as a heritage place in Alice Springs.
2. The church sits beside Adelaide House, giving the route a visible Flynn-linked pair.
3. NT Heritage Register material identifies Arthur Philpott as designer.
4. The foundation stone was laid by Prime Minister R. G. Menzies in June 1954.
5. The official opening took place on 5 May 1956.
6. Church-history sources describe the building as erected by the Presbyterian Church.
7. The building was named in honour of Rev John Flynn.
8. Flynn was a Presbyterian minister associated with the Australian Inland Mission and the Royal Flying Doctor Service.
9. The church followed the 1926 Adelaide House hospital by roughly three decades.
10. The church can be read from Todd Mall without interior access.
11. Interior material may include "Mantle of Safety" imagery or dedication language, but that needs field verification before script use.
12. The memorial framing risks heroic simplification unless the script separates evidence from institutional myth.
13. The church belongs in the route because it shows how practical systems become civic memory.
14. Its position beside Adelaide House lets the listener compare service architecture with memorial architecture.
15. The 1950s date places the church in a later phase of Alice Springs civic consolidation, after rail arrival, WWII, and postwar institutional growth.

## Script Spine

Open with the listener looking from Adelaide House to the church. Establish the visible contrast: one Flynn-linked building was designed as a hot-climate hospital in 1926; the other was built three decades later as a memorial church. Use the 1954 foundation stone and 1956 opening to explain how inland medicine, Presbyterian networks, and RFDS memory became a civic landmark. End by turning toward the Old Courthouse and Residency precinct, where institutional authority becomes government and law rather than church and medicine.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not repeat the Adelaide House hospital story.
- Do not use heroic phrases such as "mantle of safety" without explaining who the institution did and did not serve.
- Do not assume interior details are visible from the street.
- Do not let the stop become a general John Flynn biography.
- Do not confuse memorial purpose with neutral public history; institutional memory has a point of view.

## Source Checks Still Needed

- Confirm the exact visible plaque/foundation-stone text from the footpath.
- Confirm whether the interior mural can be viewed by ordinary visitors.
- Check whether Uniting Church / local congregation pages provide current access details.
- Find an authoritative source for opening-day participants beyond church-history summaries.
- Source-check Aboriginal patient access and mission history before using any broad claim about service to the inland.
