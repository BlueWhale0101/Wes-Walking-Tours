# Research Pack 09: Anzac Hill / Untyeyetwelye

Working research packet for Stop 9 of the Alice Springs / Mparntwe walking guide. This is not a script. It is the evidence base and field diagnostic to use before drafting.

## Stop Working Title

Anzac Hill / Untyeyetwelye: Memory Above the Grid

## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- Anzac Hill Memorial and plaques.
- Summit view over Alice Springs.
- Todd River / Lhere Mparntwe corridor.
- Rail corridor and Railway Terrace direction.
- Roads, town grid, civic core, and Heavitree Gap / Ntaripe direction.
- East and West MacDonnell Ranges.
- 2015 memorial walk sculptures in weathered steel and stainless steel if approached on foot.

### Core System

Public memory, military logistics, town-reading from elevation, and Arrernte place naming. The stop should use the view to synthesize the route, not simply admire scenery.

### Central Pressure

A hill significant to Arrernte people as Untyeyetwelye became a war memorial and civic lookout. From the same summit, the listener can see the dry river, rail corridor, road alignments, range gaps, and town grid that shaped the whole guide.

### Human Pressure

- NT Tourism says the Anzac Hill Memorial was unveiled on 25 April 1934.
- It was originally dedicated to armed service members who died in World War I.
- It now commemorates service in all wars in which Australia has participated.
- NT Tourism says Anzac Hill is significant to local Arrernte people, who know it as Untyeyetwelye.
- Tourism Central Australia connects Anzac Hill to the Darwin Overland Maintenance Force camp during WWII.
- Alice Springs Town Council identifies the 2015 Anzac Hill Memorial Walk artists as David Rilstone, Plazart, and Creative Metalwork.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. Anzac Hill Memorial was unveiled on 25 April 1934.
2. The original dedication was to members of the armed services who died in World War I.
3. The memorial now commemorates all who served in defence of Australia in wars in which Australia has participated.
4. NT Tourism states that Anzac Hill is significant to local Arrernte people.
5. NT Tourism gives the Arrernte name Untyeyetwelye.
6. Tourism Central Australia says Anzac Hill became home to the Darwin Overland Maintenance Force camp in 1939 on one page and 1940 on another; final script should verify date before precise use.
7. The DOMF was tied to travel and supply along the Overland Telegraph track toward Darwin.
8. Tourism Central Australia says the Overland Telegraph track upgrade was planned/funded in 1939.
9. Tourism Central Australia says the track had become a gravel road by 1941.
10. The first convoy of trucks carrying troops from Alice Springs left for Larimah in 1941 according to Tourism Central Australia.
11. Darwin was bombed on 19 February 1942.
12. The Library and Archives NT page says the bombing killed more than 230 people and destroyed ships, buildings, and infrastructure.
13. The 2015 Anzac Hill Memorial Walk is a series of sculptural installations along Anzac Hill.
14. Alice Springs Town Council identifies the artists as David Rilstone, Plazart, and Creative Metalwork.
15. The 2015 walk uses weathered steel and stainless steel.
16. Council says the works commemorate Australian involvement in war and peacekeeping missions.
17. The summit view should let the listener connect Todd River, rail corridor, road alignments, civic grid, and the MacDonnell Ranges.
18. The hill can also function as a route decision point: final stop, or skip/descend depending on heat and daylight.

## Script Spine

Open with the memorial itself, then deliberately turn the listener outward to the town. Use the hill's dual status - Untyeyetwelye and Anzac memorial - to avoid treating the lookout as neutral scenery. Move from the 1934 memorial to WWII logistics, then use the view to compress the entire guide: riverbed, rail, roads, civic institutions, hospitals, schools, and range gaps. End the guide with a practical descent instruction and a heat/safety note.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not make this a generic "best view in town" stop.
- Do not treat Arrernte naming as decorative.
- Do not use restricted cultural detail.
- Do not overstate the DOMF date until the 1939/1940 discrepancy is resolved.
- Do not let war memory crowd out the landscape-reading function.
