# Research Pack 07: Todd River Edge

Working research packet for Stop 7 of the Alice Springs / Mparntwe walking guide. This is not a script. It is the evidence base and field diagnostic to use before drafting.

## Stop Working Title

Todd River: Dry Bed, Registered Site, Flood Route

## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- Dry sand bed or flowing water depending on conditions.
- River red gums and bank vegetation.
- Causeway, bridge, low-water crossing, drains, signs, and road edges.
- Erosion cuts, sediment, tyre or foot tracks, and flood debris if present.
- Sightlines toward Heavitree Gap / Ntaripe and the MacDonnell Ranges where available.

### Core System

Water scarcity, flood risk, cultural protection, river health, erosion, roads, and public management. The stop should show that an apparently dry river governs Alice Springs physically, culturally, and administratively.

### Central Pressure

The Todd River / Lhere Mparntwe is often visibly dry, but it still controls road closures, aquifer recharge, erosion management, sacred-site obligations, shade habitat, and public behaviour. The field contradiction is the dry bed that has the power of a flood route and registered site.

### Human Pressure

- The 2019 Lhere Mparntwe Management Strategy was developed by a working group including Lhere Artepe Aboriginal Corporation, Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority, Alice Springs Landcare, Arid Lands Environment Centre, Alice Springs Town Council, Northern Territory Government, and community representatives.
- The strategy aims to manage the Todd and Charles Rivers in a coordinated and collaborative way.
- Strategy actions include developing an Mparntwe Ranger Group in accordance with the aspirations of Mparntwe sacred site custodians.
- The strategy calls for coordination between flood mitigation planning and river management.
- Council and local reporting indicate ongoing resourcing pressure around river care, but use current council documents before final script.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. NT Government describes the Lhere Mparntwe Management Strategy 2019 as a strategy for the Todd and Charles Rivers.
2. The strategy aims to manage the rivers in a coordinated and collaborative way.
3. Its stated goal is for the rivers to be ecologically healthy and recognised as iconic symbols of Alice Springs with cultural, environmental, social, and economic values.
4. The strategy says it will protect and enhance natural and cultural values.
5. It encourages culturally and ecologically appropriate recreation and relaxation.
6. It requires local and Territory governments, Traditional Owners, business, and the community to work collaboratively.
7. The working group included Lhere Artepe Aboriginal Corporation.
8. The working group included Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority.
9. The working group included Alice Springs Landcare, Arid Lands Environment Centre, Alice Springs Town Council, and NT Government.
10. Strategy actions call for an ongoing advisory group to monitor implementation.
11. Strategy actions call for commitment by individuals, schools, and community groups in managing fuel loads in small river sections.
12. Strategy actions call for support for an Mparntwe Ranger Group in accordance with aspirations of Mparntwe sacred site custodians.
13. Strategy actions note a flood mitigation geomorphologic study of the Todd River.
14. Strategy actions say flood mitigation planning should be coordinated with the Lhere Mparntwe Strategy.
15. Strategy actions call for sedimentation, erosion, and vegetation control considerations in flood mitigation.
16. Australian Government water material describes Ntaripe / Heavitree Gap as a hydrological feature where the ephemeral Todd River flows through the Gap and floods recharge alluvial aquifers.
17. Heritage Alice Springs identifies a town-centre River Red Gum as evidence of past river flood levels and says it is sacred to Arrernte people and protected under the Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority.
18. The stop should avoid restricted cultural detail and use only public management and registered/protected-site language.

## Script Spine

Open with the listener looking down into the riverbed rather than across it as scenery. Name the paradox: dry sand and gums can look inactive, but the river drives flood planning, road crossings, aquifer recharge, sacred-site management, and public maintenance. Move from visible sand, gums, drains, and causeways to the Lhere Mparntwe Strategy. Close by directing the listener toward the rail corridor, where another dry-country problem becomes visible: moving supplies across distance.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not tell restricted sacred stories.
- Do not enter the riverbed as an instruction.
- Do not treat "dry river" as a novelty joke.
- Do not make climate or flood claims without source support.
- Do not overstate river-management success; the evidence points to ongoing coordination and resourcing pressure.

## Source Checks Still Needed
