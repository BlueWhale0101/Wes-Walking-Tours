Stop Diagnostic

Visible Anchor

• Two-storey stone Adelaide House Museum building.
• Wide verandahs.
• Roofline and ventilation features.
• Todd Mall frontage.
• Nearby John Flynn Memorial Church, useful as a later memorial/institutional contrast.
• If open, the interior may help with the cellar/cooling ducts and engine room/radio experiment story.

Core System

Remote medicine as a built system: architecture, cooling, electricity, communication, church networks, medical access, and distance. The stop should explain that a hospital in Central Australia had to solve more than clinical treatment. It had to make heat bearable, move air without modern air conditioning, create power, and connect isolated people to medical advice across distance.

Central Pressure

Adelaide House was a hospital designed for a place where heat, sparse settlement, poor transport, and communication distance were part of the medical problem. The visible verandahs, stone construction, ventilated roof, and cellar-duct cooling story should carry the argument before Rev John Flynn or RFDS history enters.

Human Pressure

• Rev John Flynn designed the building and is closely associated with its construction and later aerial medical service history.
• Alfred Traeger used the engine room for a field-radio telegram experiment on 25 November 1926.
• Tourism Central Australia links Adelaide House to the Royal Flying Doctor Service’s planning and construction context.
• Heritage Alice Springs frames Adelaide House as the region’s only hospital until 1939.
• The script needs a source check on who was actually served, including Aboriginal patients and any limits, exclusions, or segregated medical practice. This is the stop’s major ethical/source gap.

Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. Adelaide House stands at 48 Todd Street, Alice Springs.
2. Tourism Central Australia describes it as easily accessed in the town centre, in Todd Mall, next to John Flynn Memorial Church.
3. Tourism Central Australia says it was designed by Rev John Flynn and built in 1926.
4. Tourism Central Australia describes Adelaide House as the first hospital in Alice Springs.
5. Heritage Alice Springs says Adelaide House was the town’s first hospital, built in 1926.
6. Heritage Alice Springs says it served as the region’s only hospital until 1939.
7. Tourism Central Australia describes the building as a two-storey stone structure.
8. Tourism Central Australia identifies wide verandahs as part of the climate design.
9. Tourism Central Australia identifies a ventilated roof as part of the climate design.
10. Tourism Central Australia describes an underground-cellar cooling system where incoming air was cooled in a cellar, circulated by ducts, and extracted through the upper storey.
11. Heritage Alice Springs describes the cooling system as unique and based on the “Coolgardie safe.”
12. Tourism Central Australia says Adelaide House was the first building in Alice Springs powered by electricity.
13. Tourism Central Australia says the building is closely associated with Rev John Flynn, its aero-medical offshoot, and the Royal Flying Doctor Service of Australia.
14. Tourism Central Australia says the RFDS was involved in planning and construction.
15. Tourism Central Australia says Alfred Traeger made one of Australia’s first successful outback radio experiments in Adelaide House’s engine room.
16. Tourism Central Australia dates that field-radio telegram reception and transmission to 25 November 1926.
17. Tourism Central Australia identifies Traeger as Flynn’s radio expert and later inventor of the pedal radio.
18. Tourism Central Australia currently describes open hours as irregular/market-linked and notes reopening language that needs verification before field use.
19. The building now functions as a museum with a health-in-Central-Australia focus.
20. The adjacent John Flynn Memorial Church was later opened in 1956, giving the route a follow-on stop about memorial architecture rather than repeating the hospital story.

Script Spine

Open with the listener looking at the stone, verandahs, roofline, and Todd Mall frontage. Establish the paradox: a hospital is usually imagined as a medical institution, but here the architecture is also a heat machine, power experiment, communications site, and distance-management system. Then move through the 1926 build, Flynn’s Australian Inland Mission context, the cooling system, electricity, and Traeger’s 25 November 1926 radio experiment. End by pointing next door to John Flynn Memorial Church as the later memorial form of the same inland network.

What The Script Must Avoid

• Do not make Flynn the whole story before the building has done explanatory work.
• Do not use “mantle of safety” or RFDS heroic language without checking who was included and excluded.
• Do not overdescribe the cellar/cooling system if the listener cannot see it from the street; use the visible roof, verandahs, stone, and museum sign as anchors.
• Do not treat “remote” as an abstract adjective. Tie it to heat, power, communication, hospital scarcity, transport, and radio.
• Do not rely on museum opening hours for the core stop.

Source Checks Still Needed

• Find a primary heritage-register file for Adelaide House, if available through the NT Heritage Register or scanned heritage documents.
• Confirm whether “built in 1926” or “completed in 1927” should be used in final script; sources differ slightly.
• Verify current open hours before converting into app guidance.
• Research Aboriginal patient access, exclusions, segregation, or documented Central Australian medical policy around Adelaide House/AIM.
• Check whether any field-visible plaque describes the cooling system, electricity, or radio experiment.
