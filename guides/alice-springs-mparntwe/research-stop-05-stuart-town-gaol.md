Working research packet for Stop 5 of the Alice Springs / Mparntwe walking guide. This is not a script. It is the evidence base and field diagnostic to use before drafting.

## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- Local-stone gaol building.
- Bars, doors, enclosure, and compound edges.
- Simple design and harsh scale.
- Parsons Street location near modern civic/legal spaces.
- Any National Trust sign or key-access notice.

### Core System

Policing, confinement, flood relocation, colonial authority, and heritage reuse. The stop should explain why a town with a tiny settler population needed a gaol, why the earlier location failed, and how the building's material simplicity reflects prisoner treatment rather than quaint heritage.

### Central Pressure

The gaol was moved into town because the earlier Heavitree Gap facility had inadequate facilities and periodic Todd River flooding. The visible stone building now appears as heritage, but its original purpose was confinement under a discriminatory early twentieth-century system.

### Human Pressure

- National Trust states construction began in 1907 by local builders using district materials.
- National Trust says the design reflects harsh discriminatory treatment of prisoners in the early twentieth century.
- The gaol housed police and prisoner history in Central Australia.
- Keys for interior access are collected from Hartley Street School during opening hours, tying its current operation to volunteer heritage labour.
- More research is needed on named prisoners, police, Aboriginal incarceration, and chain/walking-to-Port-Augusta claims before script use.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. Stuart Town Gaol stands at 8 Parsons Street.
2. Construction of the gaol and police station started in 1907.
3. National Trust says local builders used materials sourced from the district.
4. The building has a simple design made of local stone.
5. National Trust says the design reflects harsh discriminatory treatment of prisoners in the early twentieth century.
6. The gaol replaced a facility adjacent to the southern side of Heavitree Gap.
7. The older facility was considered inadequate.
8. Periodic flooding of the Todd River helped prompt the move.
9. Construction of the new gaol was completed in 1909.
10. It served as the town's gaol until 1938 according to National Trust, while Heritage Alice Springs says it served until 1939.
11. Heritage Alice Springs describes it as the oldest surviving building in the Alice Springs CBD.
12. Heritage Alice Springs dates the old gaol to 1908 local-stone construction, so final script should handle 1907 start / 1909 completion carefully.
13. The building now houses memorabilia and stories relating to police and prisoner history and life in Central Australia.
14. Interior access currently depends on Hartley Street School opening hours and key collection.
15. Current National Trust access notes list Monday-Friday 10:30am-2:30pm, seasonal closures, adult fee, and key deposit, but these are operational facts to verify before field release.
16. The gaol's survival as a heritage site can be contrasted with nearby later law-court buildings.
17. Its original flood-relocation logic creates a bridge to the later Todd River stop.
18. The stop's ethical centre is incarceration, not old-building nostalgia.

## Script Spine

Open by having the listener inspect the stone, bars, and compactness of the gaol. Establish the paradox: a building now visited as heritage was designed to confine people under a rough and discriminatory system. Use the move from Heavitree Gap, the Todd River flood problem, the 1907-1909 construction dates, and local materials to show law as a logistical and environmental problem. End by making the route carry forward: the Todd River that pushed the gaol's location will soon become its own stop.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not describe the gaol as quaint, charming, or merely old.
- Do not imply the building was humane because it is small or locally made.
- Do not invent prisoner stories.
- Do not overclaim Aboriginal incarceration details without a reliable source.
- Do not depend on interior access.
