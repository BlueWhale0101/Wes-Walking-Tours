## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- Former school buildings on Hartley Street.
- Early simple classrooms with concrete floors.
- 1945 octagonal kindergarten/classroom if visible from public access.
- Verandahs, timber elements, classroom scale, museum signage.
- Relationship to Stuart Town Gaol key collection and volunteer heritage operation.

### Core System

Education, rail growth, distance, radio communication, school design, and heritage labour. The stop should show that schooling in Alice Springs was shaped by two opposite pressures: a town population growing after rail, and outlying families too scattered for ordinary classrooms.

### Central Pressure

The town needed a fixed school because the rail terminal increased population, but Central Australia also needed school beyond town. Hartley Street School therefore becomes both a local classroom and the origin point for radio lessons through School of the Air.

### Human Pressure

- National Trust says the oldest buildings were constructed in 1929 for the increasing population arriving in the railway terminal town.
- The School of the Air began teaching on 20 September 1950 from a small studio in the school.
- Between 1953 and 1965 students and teachers moved to a school at the base of Anzac Hill.
- Public sources say students included children from St Mary's Hostel; this must be handled with careful Stolen Generations/source checks before script use.
- Current operation depends on volunteer tours and reopened in March 2026, so present-day heritage access is part of the human layer.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. Hartley Street School Museum stands at 39 Hartley Street.
2. National Trust says the oldest buildings were constructed in 1929.
3. The buildings were built to cater for increasing population arriving in an important railway terminal town.
4. Heritage Alice Springs describes it as Alice Springs' first purpose-built school.
5. Heritage Alice Springs says it was established in 1930 and served until 1965.
6. In 1945, architect B. C. G. Burnett designed a new kindergarten and classroom.
7. National Trust describes the 1945 addition as octagonal.
8. The 1945 addition had wooden floors, contrasting with the concrete floor and simple design of the earlier construction.
9. National Trust says the buildings reflect changing school design requirements across periods.
10. The unusual octagonal room is open to the public as a school museum.
11. School of the Air started teaching students on 20 September 1950.
12. The first School of the Air broadcast was made from a small studio in the school.
13. Between 1953 and 1965, students and teachers moved to a new school at the base of Anzac Hill.
14. National Trust says the museum reopened in March 2026.
15. Current National Trust opening notes list Monday-Friday 10:30am-2:30pm, with seasonal closures and contact numbers, but this needs verification near release.
16. The school sits close enough to the old court/residency/gaol precinct to read education as another civic service in the town core.
17. The route can later connect the school to Anzac Hill by explaining the 1953-1965 move to the hill base.
18. The School of the Air story is a communication story that echoes Adelaide House and radio medicine.

## Script Spine

Open at the school exterior, contrasting classroom materials and shapes. Explain that the first problem was ordinary town growth after railway arrival: more families, more children, a need for a purpose-built school. Then turn the stop outward through the 1950 radio broadcast, RFDS/radio context if sourced, and the move toward Anzac Hill. Close with the warning that the nostalgic schoolhouse can hide harder histories, especially Aboriginal children and institutions such as St Mary's Hostel, which need careful evidence before the final script.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not make the stop nostalgic without pressure.
- Do not imply School of the Air solved educational inequality for everyone.
- Do not mention St Mary's Hostel in the full script without a reliable source and careful wording.
- Do not rely on interior studio access.
- Do not overload the stop with RFDS history already used at Adelaide House.
