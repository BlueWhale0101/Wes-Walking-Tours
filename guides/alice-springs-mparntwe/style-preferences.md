# Alice Springs / Mparntwe Style Preferences

Working note for continuing the Alice Springs guide after the first two scripts.

## Target Voice

- Serious museum curator or historian speaking to intelligent adults on site.
- Field audio first: return the listener's eyes to visible evidence before interpretation.
- Graduate-level prose: each paragraph should make a causal move, not just deliver a basket of nouns.
- Some wit is welcome when it sharpens the evidence, such as the Alice Todd / no springs naming anecdote.

## Script Rules

- Target 750-850 words for ordinary stops; allow 650-900 when the evidence genuinely warrants it.
- Every paragraph should contain concrete evidence before interpretation: dates, names, materials, mechanisms, institutions, costs, design choices, legal decisions, environmental constraints, or documented human use.
- Avoid school-essay signposting and meta-commentary. Do not say phrases such as "the important word," "the best scene," "this matters," "the deeper invention," or "that is the point."
- Avoid repeated "not X but Y" contrasts. Use them only when the contrast is historically exact and worth the rhythm.
- Avoid long noun strings. No sentence should lean on a chain of abstract nouns when a cause-and-effect sentence can carry the thought.
- Keep citations in notes and references, not in the spoken script, unless naming the source is itself part of the field evidence.
- Let anecdotes do historical work. Use one human scene per stop where possible, tied to the visible place and the stop's operating problem.

## Content Preferences

- Prefer mechanisms over themes: heat as a design constraint, distance as a logistics problem, water as route logic, radio as sequence, rail as supply chain, law as a building system.
- Use numbers for environmental and political pressure: temperatures, rainfall, distances, dates, costs, administrative periods, population or traffic when sourced.
- Preserve Arrernte country, names, and public cultural authority carefully. Use public, attributable sources; do not retell restricted story material as tourist colour.
- Handle race, exclusion, and colonial policy with restraint. Place difficult facts inside operating history rather than treating them as twist endings.
- Keep Flynn present where relevant, but avoid turning each stop into a Flynn hero plaque. Adelaide House uses Traeger as the primary human story; Flynn can carry later institutional/memorial context.

## Stop 2 Lessons To Carry Forward

- The stronger Adelaide House draft emerged after adding quantified climate pressure and making Alfred Traeger a specific person through two scenes: the generator bargain and the 25 November 1926 radio test.
- The weaker version over-explained its own interpretation with source-name sentences and thesis punchlines.
- The next stops should preserve the research density while smoothing spoken narration into adult field prose.
