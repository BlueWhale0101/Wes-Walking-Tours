## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- The Residency exterior, verandahs, shaded edges, and corner siting.
- Old Courthouse exterior and court/government frontage.
- Street relationship between Parsons Street, Hartley Street, and nearby law/civic precinct.
- Materials designed for hot arid conditions: concrete floors, hollow concrete bricks, verandahs, and shade if visible or described on signage.
- Any interpretive plaques identifying Central Australia, Government Resident, or courthouse history.

### Core System

Arid-zone administration and law. The stop should show how a small inland town was made to carry formal government authority: residence, court, administrative offices, climate-adapted construction, wartime relocation, and later heritage reuse.

### Central Pressure

Central Australia was small, remote, hot, and difficult to govern from Darwin or Adelaide, yet the Commonwealth built a formal administrative and legal precinct. The visible buildings claim permanence, but their history is full of temporary arrangements: a brief separate Central Australia administration, Supreme Court status that did not actually mean local Supreme Court sittings at first, WWII relocation after Darwin was bombed, and later conversion into heritage space.

### Human Pressure

- John Charles Cawood was appointed the inaugural Government Resident of Central Australia in 1926.
- The Residency served as Cawood's official home and later as a social, administrative, and vice-regal hub.
- The building later housed the NT Administrator between 1942 and 1945 after Darwin was bombed.
- Queen Elizabeth II and the Duke of Edinburgh stayed at The Residency for two nights in March 1963.
- The Old Courthouse was where decisions were made that affected life in Central Australia, "for better or worse" in NT Tourism's wording.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. The Residency stands at the corner of Parsons and Hartley Streets.
2. The Residency represents the 1926-1931 period when Central Australia was administered separately under the North Australia Act.
3. John Charles Cawood was appointed Government Resident of Central Australia in 1926.
4. Work on The Residency began in 1926.
5. Araluen says The Residency was designed specifically for an arid climate.
6. Its concrete floors were laid directly onto the earth for passive heating and cooling.
7. Its concrete bricks were hollow to provide insulation.
8. Heritage Alice Springs says The Residency was completed in 1928 and served as Cawood's official home.
9. After repeal of the North Australia Act in 1931, it continued as the Assistant Administrator's residence.
10. Between 1942 and 1945, after Darwin was bombed, it housed the Administrator of the Northern Territory.
11. Queen Elizabeth II and the Duke of Edinburgh stayed there on 14 and 15 March 1963.
12. The Old Courthouse was opened in 1928.
13. The Old Courthouse was designed for the arid zone environment.
14. NT Tourism says construction began in 1927 and the building was sufficiently complete for occupation by November 1928.
15. The Local Court of Alice Springs had been proclaimed in 1892, but its offices were originally at the Telegraph Station.
16. From 1929 until 1931, the Old Courthouse was technically the Supreme Court of Central Australia.
17. The NT Supreme Court history notes that the Supreme Court of Central Australia only ever sat in Darwin because Alice Springs was too small and remote at the time.
18. The Old Courthouse ceased operating as a courthouse in 1980 when the new Alice Springs Law Court Building opened.
19. The new 1980 law courts cost about $3 million according to the NT Supreme Court history page.
20. The current Supreme Court building opened in 2017, giving the script a possible sightline to changing legal scale if visible from the stop.

## Script Spine

Start outside, with the listener reading climate adaptations before government titles. Then explain the brief Central Australia administrative experiment: a Government Resident, residence, court, and offices in a town still negotiating distance, heat, rail growth, and legal authority. Use WWII relocation and the 1963 Royal Visit as evidence that the building performed power theatrically as well as administratively. End by walking toward Stuart Town Gaol, where law becomes confinement rather than offices and ceremonies.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not turn this into a list of offices and dates.
- Do not overstate the Supreme Court of Central Australia; sources say the institution existed, but the court did not initially sit locally.
- Do not treat colonial administration as neutral civic progress.
- Do not rely on interiors being open.
- Do not let the royal-visit material become a cute aside; use it only if it explains vice-regal performance and the building's role.
