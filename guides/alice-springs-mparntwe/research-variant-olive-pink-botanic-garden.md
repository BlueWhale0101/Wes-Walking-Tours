# Research Pack Variant: Olive Pink Botanic Garden

Working research packet for the landscape-forward Alice Springs / Mparntwe route variant. This is not a script. It is the evidence base and field diagnostic to use before deciding whether Olive Pink replaces a core town stop.

## Stop Working Title

Olive Pink Botanic Garden: Arid Plants Under the Ranges

## Practical Field Setup

| Field Item | Draft Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Likely standing point | Garden entry off Tuncks Road, then a second pause on or below Tharrarletneme / Annie Meyers Hill if heat, mobility, and access allow. |
| Practical reason | The garden adds living arid-zone evidence and a ranges view, solving a weakness in a building-heavy town route. |
| Navigation label | Olive Pink Botanic Garden, Tuncks Road. |
| Interior dependency | None, but opening hours matter because the garden is gated. |
| Route note | Strong candidate to replace Hartley Street School or Railway Terrace in a landscape-forward version. |

## Stop Diagnostic

### Visible Anchor

- Garden entry, paths, arid-zone plantings, signs, shade, and visitor facilities.
- Native Central Australian plant collections.
- Indigenous bushfood and medicine plant interpretation if field-visible.
- Todd River proximity.
- Tharrarletneme / Annie Meyers Hill.
- View toward Alice Springs and the MacDonnell Ranges from the hill route.
- Birds, reptiles, or Black-footed Rock Wallaby / Euro habitat if observed, but do not script wildlife promises.

### Core System

Arid-zone botany, public garden-making, cultural site protection, town-edge ecology, plant knowledge, and visitor heat management. This stop should make the arid zone legible as a living system rather than empty desert scenery.

### Central Pressure

The town-centre route risks becoming too building-heavy. Olive Pink lets the guide put living plants, heat, soil, water, hill shade, sacred-site significance, and ranges views into the field. The tradeoff is route stretch, garden hours, and heat exposure.

### Human Pressure

- Olive Pink was the founder and central advocate for the reserve/garden.
- NT Heritage Register says the site was originally established as the Arid Region Flora Reserve in 1956, chiefly through Miss Pink's efforts.
- NT Tourism says the garden includes Tharrarletneme / Annie Meyers Hill, a Registered Sacred Site significant to Arrernte people.
- NT Tourism says many Indigenous people use the garden to teach and learn about local plants and traditional uses.
- The garden is also a working public institution, with curator/contact details, facilities, and daily opening hours listed by NT Tourism.

## Evidence Inventory

Use these as script-grade facts only where the visible field supports them.

1. Olive Pink Botanic Garden is located off Tuncks Road near the Alice Springs CBD.
2. NT Tourism describes it as an arid-zone botanic garden.
3. NT Tourism says it showcases more than 600 plant species in Central Australia.
4. NT Tourism says the collection includes Indigenous bushfood and medicine plants.
5. The garden includes Tharrarletneme / Annie Meyers Hill.
6. NT Tourism identifies Tharrarletneme / Annie Meyers Hill as a Registered Sacred Site significant to Arrernte people.
7. NT Tourism says many Indigenous people use the garden to teach and learn about local plants and traditional uses.
8. NT Tourism says the hill route gives views of Alice Springs and the MacDonnell Ranges.
9. NT Tourism says 89 bird species have been observed in the garden.
10. NT Tourism says visitors may encounter Black-footed Rock Wallaby, Euro, or reptiles, but this should not be promised in the audio.
11. NT Tourism lists daily opening hours of 8am-6pm.
12. NT Tourism lists free entry.
13. Listed facilities include cafe, public toilet, shop/gift shop, lawn/gardens, carpark, and free wifi.
14. Tourism Central Australia says the garden was developed as a reserve for arid-region flora by Miss Olive Pink in the 1950s.
15. NT Heritage Register says the site was originally established as the Arid Region Flora Reserve in 1956.
16. NT Heritage Register says it is Australia's oldest public garden devoted to Australian arid-region flora.
17. The garden opened to the public in 1985 according to the garden's own summary as reflected in search results; confirm with a fully accessible source before script use.
18. The garden was renamed Olive Pink Botanic Garden in 1996 according to Landscape Australia; use only if a stronger local source confirms.
19. The route impact is real: adding the garden may make the walk less compact and more heat-exposed.

## Script Spine

Open at the garden entry with visible plants, shade, paths, and signs. Establish the reason for the detour: Alice Springs is not just a town of stone institutions; it sits in an arid plant community shaped by heat, water, soil, and cultural knowledge. Move carefully to Tharrarletneme / Annie Meyers Hill using only public registered-site language. Use the view to connect garden, town, Todd River, and MacDonnell Ranges. End with a route decision: return to town or continue to Anzac Hill if conditions are good.

## What The Script Must Avoid

- Do not turn the stop into a generic garden break.
- Do not promise wildlife sightings.
- Do not narrate restricted sacred-site detail.
- Do not rely on cafe/open facilities without current verification.
- Do not add Olive Pink to the core route unless the route length and heat burden still fit the field job.

## Source Checks Still Needed

- Confirm current garden opening hours directly before guide release.
- Field-check walk time from Todd River / Todd Mall and heat exposure on Tuncks Road.
- Find a stronger primary source for Olive Pink biography, 1985 opening, and 1996 renaming.
- Identify plant-specific visible signs or species for the eventual script.
- Confirm whether the garden permits public interpretation/audio use along all intended paths.
