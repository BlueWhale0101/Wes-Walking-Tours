# Alice Springs / Mparntwe Stop List Draft

Working draft for an Alice Springs walking guide, following `docs/guide-authoring.md` and `docs/script-production.md`.

## Field Job

| Question | Draft Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who is walking? | Adult visitor with limited local knowledge, likely on foot from the town centre. The listener wants visible evidence, not tourist filler. |
| How much time is available? | Core route: 85-110 minutes including stops, shade pauses, and the Anzac Hill climb. Keep the walk compact enough that a visitor can use it from the town centre without needing a car, bike, or exposed long transfer. |
| What can change today? | Heat, smoke/fire season, Todd River flood/road closures, museum opening hours, safety conditions in the town centre, and access to heritage interiors. |
| Intellectual spine | Mparntwe as a dry-river town where Arrernte country, water, communications logistics, rail growth, civic administration, policing, remote medicine, schooling, war infrastructure, and tourism all become visible in a compact town centre. |
| Where will signal fail? | Field use may be heat-stressed even in the town centre, and museum interiors may be closed when the user arrives. Core guide should cache map, audio, and references locally before field use. |

## Recommended Guide Shape

Start with a compact town-centre loop. Drop the Telegraph Station from this guide rather than treating it as an optional tail; it is a strong colonial-origin site, but it pulls the visitor out of the town-centre field job and adds a long exposed transfer. The next-best town-centre replacement is John Flynn Memorial Church because it is directly beside Adelaide House, has NT Heritage Register support, and extends the remote-medicine/church network from the 1926 hospital into a 1950s civic memorial building.

Olive Pink Botanic Garden and a deliberate MacDonnell Ranges view are both strong candidates for the route. Anzac Hill should remain the core guide's main ranges-view stop because it is central, walkable, and lets the visitor read the town grid, Todd River, rail corridor, Heavitree Gap direction, and East/West MacDonnell Ranges from one place. Olive Pink Botanic Garden / Annie Meyer Hill should be treated as the strongest ecology variant: it adds arid-zone plants, a second hill view, and a living-garden field stop, but it may push the route beyond the compact town-centre job in hot weather.

- **Core guide:** Todd Mall / civic heritage / Todd River / Railway Terrace / Anzac Hill loop, about 3.0-3.8 km depending on the exact river crossing and hill route. This is the recommended first build.
- **Landscape-forward variant:** Replace Hartley Street School and Railway Terrace with Todd River / Olive Pink Botanic Garden / Anzac Hill. This makes the route more about arid ecology, shade, plants, and the town's setting inside the MacDonnell Ranges, while avoiding a zigzag from the river to the garden, back west to the railway, and then north to Anzac Hill.
- **Dropped from this guide:** Telegraph Station Historical Reserve. Save it for a future, separate guide only if the project explicitly wants a longer reserve walk.
- **Draft status:** Keep the guide `"draft"` until route coordinates, current safety/access notes, map asset, audio, and any local images are in place.

## Proposed Core Route

Sequence decision: keep the core route as a compact town-centre walk that moves from Todd Mall institutions, through the civic/heritage precinct, east to the Todd River, west to the railway corridor, and then north/up to Anzac Hill. This order is convenient because it avoids making the visitor climb Anzac Hill before the synthesis stop, keeps adjacent Flynn/Adelaide House material together, and uses the final hill view to summarize the river, rail, roads, town grid, and ranges.

## Landscape-Forward Route Variant

If the guide needs a stronger natural-history and ranges-view component, promote Olive Pink Botanic Garden / Annie Meyer Hill into the main sequence after the Todd River stop, then finish at Anzac Hill only if the visitor has enough time, water, and daylight. Do not treat Olive Pink as a casual tail after Anzac Hill; it works best as a deliberate route variant from the river side.

## Stop Notes And Source Hooks

### 1. `mparntwe-field-briefing` - Field Briefing: A Town Inside Country

- **Visible anchor:** Todd Mall, pedestrian paving, public art, shopfront shade, street trees, view lines toward Anzac Hill or the ranges.
- **Core system:** Orientation, cultural authority, heat, water, and field safety.
- **Central pressure:** A visitor wants a compact town walk, but the visible town sits inside Arrernte country, extreme heat, dry-river hydrology, and live civic/safety conditions.
- **Human evidence to confirm:** Lhere Artepe and Alice Springs Town Council wording for Traditional Owners and Mparntwe; any public art source for Yeperenye/caterpillar imagery in Todd Mall.
- **Likely references:** Alice Springs Town Council history page; Lhere Artepe cultural protocols/about pages; Alice Springs public art map.
- **Weakness risk:** Becoming a generic acknowledgement. The script must point to visible public art, hills, river direction, signs, and route decisions.

### 2. `adelaide-house` - Adelaide House: Heat, Medicine, and Distance

- **Visible anchor:** Two-storey stone building, verandahs, roofline, vents/cooling story, Todd Mall frontage.
- **Core system:** Remote medicine as architecture plus communication infrastructure.
- **Central pressure:** A hospital in a remote inland town had to solve heat, distance, electricity, radio communication, and shortage of medical access at once.
- **Evidence already found:** Designed by Rev John Flynn; built in 1926; first hospital in Alice Springs; designed for hot inland climate; wide verandahs, ventilated roof, underground-cellar cooling ducts; first building in Alice Springs powered by electricity; Alfred Traeger field-radio telegram experiment on 25 November 1926; connected to RFDS planning/history.
- **Likely references:** NT Tourism Adelaide House page; Heritage Alice Springs; Uniting Church/Flynn sources; 2024 Adelaide House research article if accessible.
- **Weakness risk:** Flattening Flynn into a hero plaque. Need include who was and was not served, and source-check racial limits of the "mantle of safety."

### 3. `john-flynn-memorial-church` - John Flynn Memorial Church: A Memorial Built for the Inland

- **Visible anchor:** Church exterior beside Adelaide House, plaques/foundation stone if visible, masonry, shade, roofline, Todd Mall relationship.
- **Core system:** Civic memory, church networks, memorial fundraising, arid-climate public architecture, and institutional authority.
- **Central pressure:** The same Flynn-linked network that built a 1926 practical hospital later built a 1950s memorial church, turning remote medicine and Presbyterian mission into a permanent town-centre landmark.
- **Evidence already found:** NT Heritage Register identifies the church as designed by Arthur Philpott, with foundation stone laid by Prime Minister R.G. Menzies in June 1954 and official opening on 5 May 1956; Heritage Alice Springs places the church four years after John Flynn's Grave was erected in 1952.
- **Likely references:** NT Heritage Register John Flynn Memorial Church; Heritage Alice Springs; Alice Springs Uniting Church; NLA opening and dedication program if accessible.
- **Weakness risk:** Repeating Adelaide House. This stop should not retell the hospital; it should ask how an institution turns a practical inland service into architecture, memorial plaques, and public authority.

### 4. `old-courthouse-residency` - Court and Residency: Governing the Centre

- **Visible anchor:** Old Courthouse, The Residency, government building materials, verandahs, Hartley/Parsons street relationship.
- **Core system:** Law, arid-zone administration, and Central Australia's brief separate government status.
- **Central pressure:** The town was small, remote, and difficult to supply, yet built formal institutions that claimed legal and administrative permanence.
- **Evidence already found:** Northern Australia Act period 1926-1931; John Charles Cawood appointed Government Resident in 1926; The Residency designed for arid climate with hollow concrete bricks and concrete floors laid directly on earth; NT administration moved there 1942-1945 after bombing of Darwin; Queen Elizabeth II and Duke of Edinburgh stayed two nights during 1963 visit; Old Courthouse opened/completed around 1928 and operated until 1980.
- **Likely references:** Araluen Residency page; NT Tourism Old Courthouse page; NT Supreme Court history; Heritage Alice Springs.
- **Weakness risk:** Too much institutional summary. The script must make cooling, materials, verandahs, court frontage, and street proximity do the explanatory work.

### 5. `stuart-town-gaol` - Stuart Town Gaol: Law in a Flood Town

- **Visible anchor:** Stone gaol at 8 Parsons Street, bars, enclosure, nearby legal/civic precinct.
- **Core system:** Policing, confinement, flood relocation, settler authority, and civic reuse.
- **Central pressure:** The town's law infrastructure moved because the earlier gaol near Heavitree Gap had inadequate facilities and flooding problems, yet the new gaol also needs Indigenous incarceration and policing context.
- **Evidence already found:** New gaol completed 1909; replaced facility on southern side of Heavitree Gap; decision prompted by inadequate facilities and periodic Todd River flooding; served as town gaol until 1938/1939; now National Trust site with memorabilia; claimed oldest surviving CBD building by Heritage Alice Springs.
- **Likely references:** National Trust Stuart Town Gaol; Heritage Alice Springs; NT Heritage Register if available; policing/Aboriginal incarceration sources.
- **Weakness risk:** Treating the gaol as quaint heritage. Must include documented human pressure and avoid evasive language around incarceration.

### 6. `hartley-street-school` - Hartley Street School: Teaching a Scattered Town

- **Visible anchor:** Former school buildings at Hartley Street, verandahs, concrete floors, 1945 octagonal kindergarten/classroom.
- **Core system:** Education, distance, rail growth, radio schooling, and Aboriginal student history.
- **Central pressure:** A town with railway-era population growth and remote families needed a fixed school, then used radio to reach children beyond town.
- **Evidence already found:** Oldest buildings constructed 1929; opened 1930; first official/purpose-built school building in Alice Springs; 1945 kindergarten and classroom by architect B.C.G. Burnett; School of the Air first broadcast from here on 20 September 1950; RFDS base helped relay transmission; students included children from St Mary's Hostel, requiring careful source checks.
- **Likely references:** National Trust Hartley Street School; NT Heritage Register; Discover Central Australia; School of the Air history; St Mary's Hostel sources.
- **Weakness risk:** Nostalgia. The script needs overcrowding, broadcast acoustics, forced distance, and Aboriginal student context.

### 7. `todd-river-edge` - Todd River: Dry Bed, Registered Site, Flood Route

- **Visible anchor:** Sand bed, river red gums, causeway/bridge, drains, warning signs, riverbank erosion, road crossings.
- **Core system:** Water scarcity, flood risk, sacred-site management, erosion, roads, and public behaviour.
- **Central pressure:** The Todd is often visibly dry, but it governs town crossings, emergency closures, river health work, and cultural obligations.
- **Evidence already found:** Alice Springs Town Council and NT/council documents reference the Lhere Mparntwe Management Strategy for Todd and Charles Rivers; council meeting material describes active erosion areas and flood-related damage; an AAPA-related council minute says the Todd River between the Gap and 300 m north of Schwartz Crescent had been declared a registered sacred site; recent flood events close low-lying roads and draw public spectators.
- **Likely references:** Lhere Mparntwe Management Strategy; Alice Springs Town Council environment committee minutes; NT road/flood safety sources; Bureau of Meteorology only for current field safety.
- **Weakness risk:** Overclaiming cultural detail without permission. Use public registered-site and management-language only; do not narrate restricted sacred stories.

### 8. `railway-and-ghan` - Railway Terrace: Camels, Rail, and the Inland Supply Chain

- **Visible anchor:** Railway station, rail corridor, Railway Terrace cottages/industrial edges, road/rail alignment.
- **Core system:** Transport logistics: camel, goldfield supply, rail, road, tourism.
- **Central pressure:** The town changed when supply moved from camel trains and a distant railhead to a railway terminal, but rail arrived after telegraph and gold had already forced the inland network.
- **Evidence already found:** Arltunga gold discovered in 1887, about 110 km east; camel trains carried essential provisions 600 km from the Oodnadatta railhead; journey east toward Arltunga took at least a week and temperatures often exceeded 40 C; railway reached Stuart in 1929; first "Afghan Express" left Adelaide 4 August and arrived 6 August; The Ghan transcontinental route completed February 2004 and covers 2979 km in 54 hours.
- **Likely references:** Alice Springs Town Council history page; Heritage Alice Springs; rail/Old Ghan sources.
- **Weakness risk:** Becoming a transport timeline detached from the field. Need point to track alignment, platform, shade, freight edges, and the town grid.

### 9. `anzac-hill` - Anzac Hill / Untyeyetwelye: Memory Above the Grid

- **Visible anchor:** Anzac Hill Memorial, climb, summit view, memorial plaques/sculptural walk, town grid, Todd River, rail corridor, MacDonnell Ranges.
- **Core system:** Public memory, military logistics, town-reading from elevation, and Arrernte place naming.
- **Central pressure:** A hill known to Arrernte people as Untyeyetwelye became a war memorial and lookout where the visitor can see the town's river, rail, roads, and ranges compressed into one field of view.
- **Evidence already found:** Memorial unveiled on 25 April 1934; originally dedicated to those who died in World War I; now commemorates Australian service in all wars; NT Tourism identifies Anzac Hill as significant to local Arrernte people, who know it as Untyeyetwelye; Discover Central Australia says the Darwin Overland Maintenance Force camp was at the hill from 1939; Alice Springs public art page identifies 2015 Anzac Hill Memorial Walk sculptures in weathered steel and stainless steel.
- **Likely references:** NT Tourism Anzac Hill; Discover Central Australia; Alice Springs Town Council public art map; military/RSL source if available.
- **Weakness risk:** The hill can become a generic "great view" stop. Script must make the view do analytical work: river crossings, rail/road corridor, gaps, ranges, civic grid, memorial text.

### Variant. `olive-pink-botanic-garden` - Olive Pink Botanic Garden: Arid Plants Under the Ranges

- **Visible anchor:** Garden entrance off Tuncks Road, arid-zone plantings, paths, shade structures, Todd River proximity, Annie Meyer Hill lookout, town/ranges view.
- **Core system:** Arid-zone botany, public garden-making, town-edge ecology, and the habit of seeing Central Australia as a living plant community rather than empty red space.
- **Central pressure:** A walking guide about Alice Springs can become too building-heavy; the botanic garden lets the visitor read heat, water, soil, shade, birds, and ranges through living evidence.
- **Evidence already found:** NT Tourism lists Olive Pink Botanic Garden as open daily 8am-6pm with free entry, public toilets, cafe, lawn/garden facilities, and wheelchair-accessible visitor support; the garden's own visitor page confirms 7-day opening except Good Friday, Christmas Day, and New Year's Day; Tourism Central Australia describes Anzac Hill as a central lookout with full-town, MacDonnell Ranges, and desert views.
- **Likely references:** Olive Pink Botanic Garden visitor page; NT Tourism Olive Pink Botanic Garden; Tourism Central Australia Olive Pink Botanic Garden; Discover Central Australia Anzac Hill; NT Tourism East and West MacDonnell Ranges pages for broader ranges context.
- **Weakness risk:** Turning the stop into a generic nice-garden pause. The script needs plant-specific field evidence, Annie Meyer Hill view instructions, and a clear reason the garden belongs in an Alice Springs route rather than a separate nature walk.

## Route Questions Before `guide.json`

1. Should the main route start in Todd Mall or at the Alice Springs Visitor Information Centre?
2. Should the railway stop be included in the main loop, or saved for a longer "logistics" variant?
3. Should Anzac Hill be the final stop, or should the guide descend and end back at Todd Mall for easier navigation?
4. Should John Flynn Memorial Church be a short exterior stop paired with Adelaide House, or a full stop with its own script?
5. Should Olive Pink Botanic Garden remain an optional landscape-forward variant, or become the main route by replacing Hartley Street School and Railway Terrace?
6. Which stops need interior access for images or evidence, and which must work from the street when museums are closed?
